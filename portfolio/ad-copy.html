Print Advertisement

AIDA-structured print ad targeting academic researchers.

Format: Full-page print ad for academic trade publication (e.g., The Chronicle of Higher Education)

Target audience: Dr. Maya Chen — tenure-track researcher, dictionary-based text analysis, no coding background

Ad Copy (AIDA Structure)

[ATTENTION]

Your R script broke again.

[INTEREST]

You have 1,200 survey responses, a conference deadline in six weeks and a sentiment dictionary you haven't been able to apply since your advisor's code stopped compiling. You know the analysis. You know the statistics. The only thing standing between you and your results is a programming language you were never trained to use.

[DESIRE]

TASS is a desktop application that runs dictionary-based text analysis without code, without cloud uploads and without recurring subscription fees. Import a CSV. Select a dictionary. View your results in a structured output panel with effect sizes, confidence intervals and APA-formatted tables you can export directly into your manuscript. Welch's t-test, ANOVA, Kruskal-Wallis, post-hoc comparisons, correlations — the statistics your reviewers expect, computed correctly, displayed clearly.

Every analysis runs on your machine. No data leaves your computer. No IRB amendment required.

Built by Alex Leith, PhD — a professor who teaches the methods course and got tired of watching the tools fail his students.

[ACTION]

Download the free 30-day trial at usetass.app. No credit card. No account creation. Just install and analyze.

TASS. Text Analysis Support Software.

Your data deserves better tools.

Visual concept: Clean white background. Top third: headline in bold serif type ("Your R script broke again."). Middle: body copy in two columns, professional layout. Bottom: TASS logo in green (#2E7D5E), tagline, URL, and a minimal screenshot showing the structured output viewer with an APA table. No stock photos. No clip art. The ad should look like it belongs in Harvard Business Review, not a software flyer.
