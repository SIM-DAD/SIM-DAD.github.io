TASS Launch Campaign — SIM DAD LLC

Strategic Communication Track | MC 422 | Spring 2026

SIM DAD LLC is a software company I founded in June 2024 to build local-first desktop tools for researchers, educators and knowledge workers. This portfolio documents the strategic communications campaign for the company's flagship product launch: TASS (Text Analysis Support Software), a no-code text analysis application for social scientists releasing May 31, 2026.

The campaign was built on a single strategic insight identified during the research phase: the researchers who most need dictionary-based text analysis are the least equipped to access it. The dominant tool in the market (LIWC-22) costs $200 per year per seat, requires institutional procurement and offers no built-in statistical testing. The open-source alternative (R and Python) requires programming skills that most social science graduate programs do not teach. TASS occupies the space between these two options — affordable, offline, statistically complete and code-free.

Every deliverable in this portfolio serves a defined role in a sequenced launch campaign:

  • The Backgrounder (Lab 1) established the company's credibility and product portfolio for press use.
  • The Audience Persona (Lab 2) identified Dr. Maya Chen — a tenure-track researcher who understands the methodology but cannot operate the tools — as the primary buyer.
  • The Content Calendar (Lab 3) deployed a 30-day Instagram and LinkedIn campaign anchored by the TASS launch countdown.
  • The Strategic Message Planner (Lab 4) organized the campaign around three key messages, with "Your data deserves better tools" as the positioning statement.
  • The News Release (Lab 5) announced the TASS launch in AP Style, structured for pickup by education technology and higher education press.
  • The Print Ad (Lab 6) applied the AIDA formula to reach the Lab 2 persona through trade publication placement.
  • The Executive Blog Post (Lab 7) established thought leadership on the local-first philosophy that differentiates SIM DAD from cloud-based competitors.
  • The Internal Memo (Lab 8) documented a real business disruption — the rejection of our original payment processor — and modeled transparent stakeholder communication during a vendor transition.
  • The Media Pitch (Lab 9) targeted a named technology reporter at Inside Higher Ed with a personalized angle connecting the TASS launch to her existing coverage of faculty technology adoption.
  • The Evaluation Plan (Lab 10) defined four measurable KPIs — paid subscriptions, earned media mentions, trial activation rate and social engagement — with specific success thresholds and diagnostic logic for each.

The key takeaway from this semester's work is that strategic communication does not require a large budget or a dedicated team. It requires a framework. The 9-Step Strategic Planning Process provided that framework: research grounded in direct observation, messaging tested against a real persona, distribution targeted at a specific gatekeeper and evaluation criteria that diagnose the point of failure rather than simply measuring whether the campaign "worked." Every piece in this portfolio was written to be published. Several of them will be.
