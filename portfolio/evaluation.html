Lab 10 — Step 9: Evaluating

Evaluation Plan

Four KPIs with measurement tools, benchmarks and success definitions.

Client: SIM DAD LLC

Campaign: TASS Launch (May 31, 2026)

Evaluation window: May 31 - July 31, 2026 (60 days)

Prepared by: Alex Leith, PhD

KPI 1: Conversion — Paid Subscriptions

The metric: Total paid TASS subscriptions (any tier) activated through Gumroad within 60 days of launch.

The tool: Gumroad Analytics dashboard. Gumroad tracks all purchases by product, tier, date and geography. Data is available in real time and exportable as CSV.

The benchmark: Zero. TASS has no prior sales history. The baseline is the launch itself. Industry context: comparable indie research tools (MacWhisper, Scrivener) typically report 200-500 paid users in the first 60 days with organic marketing only, based on publicly shared launch retrospectives on Hacker News and Indie Hackers.

Definition of success: 200 paid subscriptions across all tiers within 60 days. This threshold represents a revenue run rate sufficient to cover annual operating costs (domain, code signing, Gumroad fees) and validates market demand for a LIWC alternative.

KPI 2: Awareness — Earned Media Mentions

The metric: Number of articles, blog posts, newsletter mentions or podcast segments that reference TASS by name in education technology, academic research or higher education publications.

The tool: Google Alerts for "TASS text analysis" and "SIM DAD LLC," supplemented by manual monitoring of target outlets (Inside Higher Ed, EdSurge, Chronicle of Higher Education, CRTNET listserv). Mentions are logged in a spreadsheet with outlet name, date, author, URL, estimated reach and whether the mention was solicited (from our pitch) or organic.

The benchmark: Zero pre-launch mentions. Comparable academic tool launches (Qualtrics' initial academic push, Dedoose's launch coverage) generated 5-15 earned mentions within 90 days based on press archive review.

Definition of success: 10 earned media mentions within 90 days of launch (May 31 - Aug. 31, 2026), with at least 3 appearing in publications that reach our primary audience (higher education faculty and graduate students).

KPI 3: Engagement — Free Trial Activation Rate

The metric: Percentage of website visitors who download and install the free 30-day trial, as measured by Gumroad trial activations divided by unique product page visits.

The tool: Gumroad Analytics provides download counts. Website analytics (Plausible or Cloudflare Web Analytics, both privacy-respecting) provide unique page views to the TASS product page. The conversion rate is calculated as: (trial downloads / unique product page visits) x 100.

The benchmark: Industry average for free-to-paid trial conversion in SaaS products is 2-5% (Totango 2024 benchmark report). For desktop software with no credit card required at trial, initial download rates from product page visitors typically range from 8-15% (based on MacWhisper and Obsidian's publicly shared metrics). The relevant metric here is page-to-trial, not trial-to-paid.

Definition of success: A trial activation rate of 10% or higher from unique product page visitors. Below 8% suggests the product page is not effectively communicating value. Above 15% suggests strong product-market fit and effective messaging.

KPI 4: Audience Behavior — Social Media Engagement Rate

The metric: Average engagement rate across LinkedIn and Instagram posts in the TASS launch campaign (May 1-31, 2026), defined as (likes + comments + shares + saves) / impressions x 100.

The tool: LinkedIn Analytics (native, available on company pages) and Instagram Insights (native, available on business/creator accounts). Both platforms provide per-post engagement metrics. Data is compiled weekly into the campaign tracking spreadsheet.

The benchmark: LinkedIn organic engagement rate for company pages averages 2.0-3.0% across industries (Hootsuite 2025 Social Trends Report). Instagram averages 1.5-3.0% for business accounts with fewer than 10,000 followers. SIM DAD's accounts are new with minimal following, so early posts may show higher volatility.

Definition of success: Average engagement rate of 3.0% or higher on LinkedIn and 2.5% or higher on Instagram across all campaign posts. This indicates that the content resonates with the target audience (academic researchers) rather than generating passive impressions.

Rejected Metric: Website Traffic Volume

I considered total website traffic (unique visitors to sim-dad.github.io) as a KPI but rejected it because raw traffic volume does not distinguish between relevant visitors (researchers evaluating TASS) and irrelevant visitors (bots, accidental clicks, general curiosity). A product page could receive 10,000 visits and produce zero trials if the traffic source is misaligned with the target audience.

The trial activation rate (KPI 3) is a more diagnostic metric because it measures the quality of traffic — specifically, the proportion of visitors who found enough value in the product page to take the next step. High traffic with low activation signals a messaging or targeting problem. Low traffic with high activation signals a distribution problem. The activation rate separates these two failure modes, while raw traffic volume conflates them.
