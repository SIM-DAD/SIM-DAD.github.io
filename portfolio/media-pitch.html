Lab 9 — Step 6: Editing

Media Pitch

Personalized pitch to an ed-tech journalist at Inside Higher Ed.

Format: Email pitch to named gatekeeper

Pitched story: TASS v1.0 launch (from Lab 5 news release)

Target: Lauren Coffey, Technology & Innovation Reporter, Inside Higher Ed

The Pitch

TO: Lauren Coffey, lcoffey@insidehighered.com

FROM: Alex Leith, PhD, Founder — SIM DAD LLC

SUBJECT: Professor builds no-code text analysis tool after watching students struggle with R

Lauren,

Your reporting on the gap between faculty and institutional technology decisions caught my attention — particularly the tension between the tools researchers need and the tools their institutions provide. I think I have a story in that space that your readers would find relevant.

I am an associate professor of mass communications at SIUE. I teach research methods. Every semester, I watch students spend more time debugging R scripts than analyzing their data. The tools that exist for dictionary-based text analysis — primarily LIWC-22 at $200 per year per seat — require either expensive site licenses or coding skills my students were never trained to use.

So I built an alternative. TASS (Text Analysis Support Software) is a Windows desktop application that runs dictionary-based text scoring, group comparisons and APA-formatted statistical exports without requiring code or a cloud account. It launches publicly on May 31. Subscription pricing starts at $79 per year for academic users — less than half the cost of LIWC — with lab and department tiers for institutional adoption.

The angle I think matters for your audience: TASS is entirely local-first. Every analysis runs on the user's machine. No data is transmitted. This was a deliberate design choice for researchers working with IRB-protected data, student records or proprietary organizational text. The tool eliminates the compliance question that cloud-based analysis introduces.

I am available for an interview at your convenience and can provide a press copy of TASS for your own evaluation. I can also connect you with faculty at other institutions who have previewed the tool during development.

Alex Leith, PhD

Founder, SIM DAD LLC

Associate Professor, Mass Communications — SIUE

ap@simdadllc.com | (504) 534-8446

Targeting Rationale

Lauren Coffey covers technology and innovation in higher education for Inside Higher Ed. Her beat includes AI adoption, ed-tech implementation and the institutional dynamics around faculty technology decisions. Her reporting on librarian AI adoption barriers and e-textbook trust gaps demonstrates a consistent interest in the friction between available tools and institutional readiness.

Inside Higher Ed is the right outlet for this story because its readership is the TASS buyer: department chairs evaluating site-license alternatives, tenure-track faculty choosing between coding and commercial tools, and graduate students searching for affordable research infrastructure. The publication's tech-innovation vertical reaches this audience directly, and Lauren's beat aligns with the specific tension TASS addresses — the gap between what researchers need and what their institutions provide.

The pitch leads with the personal narrative (professor builds the tool he wished existed) because Lauren's coverage shows a pattern of centering individual faculty experiences within larger institutional trends. The LIWC pricing comparison and local-first angle provide the news hook that distinguishes this from a generic product announcement.
