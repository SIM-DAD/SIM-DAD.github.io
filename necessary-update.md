# SIM DAD LLC — Website Content Brief

## Strategic Reframe

The site currently presents SIM DAD as a software company with a research background. It should present SIM DAD as a research and consulting practice with a software portfolio. That is not a subtle distinction. It changes what a visitor does when they arrive: instead of clicking through to a product site, they think about whether they need to hire you.

The acronym is also doing invisible work right now. "Social and Interactive Media Data Analysis and Design" is a precise, credentialing description of what the practice covers. It should appear on the site, named and explained, not buried or omitted.

---

## Section-by-Section Recommendations

### 1. Hero (Highest Priority)

**Current:** "Local-first AI software — founded June 2024" / "Software for people who do serious work."

This is the wrong first impression for your target audience. An organization director looking for a research consulting partner, or a program officer scanning potential grant collaborators, reads this and thinks: software vendor. They bounce.

**Recommended reframe:**

The eyebrow should name the practice and its domain. Something in the register of: *"Research consulting · Data analysis · Social & interactive media"*

The headline should signal expertise and availability for engagement, not a product. The current headline sells a product ethos. You want something that sells a person and a practice — credible, specific, and inviting contact.

The subhead should explain what SIM DAD actually stands for, briefly, and describe the range of work: research design, large-scale data analysis, and the software tools that came out of that work.

The call-to-action buttons should lead with consulting/contact, not product sites. Product links can appear lower on the page.

---

### 2. About Section

**Current:** Leads with the software products. Publications and research history are downstream.

**Recommended reframe:** The about section should open with the practice: what kinds of problems SIM DAD takes on, for whom, and what distinguishes the approach (research rigor applied to applied problems; the scale of prior work). The software portfolio should be described as a natural outgrowth of that work, not the centerpiece of it. One or two sentences max on the products here, with a link to the Products section.

The founding date and "Local-first AI software" descriptor in the sidebar should be replaced or supplemented with domain expertise areas: Social VR/XR, parasocial media and streaming, HCI, NLP and computational text analysis.

---

### 3. Products Section

**Current:** The section leads the site's narrative and treats Ibis, TASS, Lector, and The Cage as the primary offerings.

**Recommended reframe:** Move this section lower in the page order, after consulting credentials have been established. Reframe the section introduction to make clear that these tools were built to solve real research problems and are now available commercially. The framing "tools we built for ourselves, now available to others" is honest and credentialing at the same time.

**On The Cage specifically:** Its ownership situation is genuinely complicated (potential 51% SIUE ownership under the CBA). Until that is resolved, the site should either omit it or describe it neutrally as an institutional deployment project rather than a product for sale. Presenting it as a commercial product before ownership is settled creates a false impression. Frame it as a consulting deliverable and proof of capability instead.

---

### 4. Track Record Section

**Current:** The section exists and is well-structured, but it reads as background context rather than a lead credential.

**Recommended reframe:** This section should move up in the page order, closer to the hero, so that consulting and grant prospects encounter the scale of prior work before they reach the products. The framing should connect each metric explicitly to what it demonstrates about SIM DAD's consulting capabilities: the 321M Twitch messages demonstrate pipeline-scale data engineering; the $1.59M NSF award demonstrates program leadership and grant track record; the 2,448+ survey respondents demonstrate survey research at scale. Right now the numbers stand alone. They need one-sentence glosses that speak to a client or program officer, not just an academic audience.

---

### 5. Publications

**Current:** Seven publications listed in full on the business site.

**Recommended reframe:** Reduce to two or three representative entries, chosen for recognizability of venue (PLOS One, IEEE, Communication Monographs) and relevance to consulting domain areas. Add a single line: *"Full publication list at apleith.com."* This keeps the credential present without making the business site feel like a CV, and it correctly routes academic audiences to the personal site.

---

### 6. Team Section

**Current:** Alex's bio is thorough and accurate. The SIUE affiliation appears in the body copy but not prominently.

**Recommended reframe:** The bio should more explicitly name the consulting offer. Something like: Alex is available for research consulting, speaking, and grant collaborations in his areas of expertise. Right now the bio reads as a credentials summary. It should end with an implied or explicit invitation. The SIUE associate professor line should appear early in the bio — for consulting and grant audiences, institutional affiliation is a trust signal.

---

### 7. Navigation and Contact

**Current:** Nav links are About, Products, Team, Contact. Contact goes to an email address.

**Recommended reframe:** Add a dedicated consulting or "Work with us" anchor or section. The contact prompt should name the kinds of engagements you are open to: research consulting, speaking, grant partnerships, software licensing. Right now a visitor does not know what to ask for. Give them the language.

---

## What to Keep As-Is

- The capabilities grid is accurate and well-organized. Keep it.
- The track record numbers are strong. Keep them; just reframe the glosses.
- The overall visual structure and section organization is sound. This is a copy and content problem, not a layout problem.
- The product descriptions for Ibis, TASS, and Lector are clear and accurate. Keep them where they are; just move them lower in the page hierarchy.

---

## Page Order Recommendation

The current order is: Hero → Products → About → Track Record → Capabilities → Team.

Recommended order: Hero → About (practice-first) → Track Record → Consulting / Work With Us → Capabilities → Products → Team.

This order tells the right story for your target audience: here is what we do, here is the evidence that we do it well, here is how to engage us, here is the supporting infrastructure, here are the tools, here are the people.

---

## One Sentence Summary

The site needs to stop opening with what SIM DAD sells and start opening with what SIM DAD knows.
